# P02 — Executive Wallboard

## Page brief

- Primary user: SOC director, CISO, business risk owner.
- Job story: review security outcomes, response capability, business exposure and data confidence to decide resourcing, risk acceptance and cross-functional commitments.
- First-screen questions:
  1. Is business risk improving or worsening against target?
  2. Which business units drive the gap?
  3. Which conclusions are limited by coverage or late data?
- Primary action: `Export management report` through the existing prototype action/receipt boundary.
- Primary data surface: one executive risk trend, business-unit pressure map and a short management-commitment table.
- KPI need: yes, but condensed to three decision outcomes rather than an analyst metric strip.
- Chart need: one management trend with an exact-data table fallback.
- Overlay: page-specific metric-definition flyout; export remains the existing governed prototype action.

## Difference contract

- Archetype: executive wallboard.
- Hero region: narrative executive posture with one decision callout and three outcome indicators.
- First viewport regions: scope controls → posture narrative → one risk trend → business-unit pressure.
- Primary action: export management report.
- Detail type: metric-definition flyout and management-report simulation.
- Must not copy: P20 ITSM Analytics.

### Three required structural differences from P20

1. P02 opens with a management narrative and risk-appetite decision; P20 opens with analysis-definition controls.
2. P02's main surface is a fixed executive trend plus business-unit pressure; P20's main surface is a configurable analysis canvas and metric contract inspector.
3. P02 interactions change reporting scope and inspect definitions/export; P20 interactions construct, run, compare and save analyses.

## Shared-component risk decision

- Generic `MetricStrip`, `MiniChart`, `QuestionRail` and `PrototypeGrid` are not used.
- EUI panels, selects, progress indicators and flyout primitives are reused only as design-system primitives.
- The business composition is page-specific and intentionally lower-density than analyst workspaces.
